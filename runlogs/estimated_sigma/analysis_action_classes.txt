=== 1. what hedging can buy, against two baselines ===
  best single constant across all levels      0.2500
  best constant chosen per level              0.2721   +0.0221
  perfect PER-FRAME hedging (oracle)          0.2735   +0.0235
  -> everything hedging can do, including a perfect per-frame choice,
     is worth +0.0235 over one constant.

=== 2. what correcting the pose buys, by how far it gets ===
  Reading down a column: if correction takes you from the row's noise
  level to the column's, and you then hedge optimally, you score the
  column's best-hedge value.

  from sigma_pos  best hedge      ->0.00   ->0.10   ->0.20   ->0.30   ->0.60
            0.10      0.2810     +0.1176                                    
            0.20      0.2506     +0.1480  +0.0304                           
            0.30      0.2407     +0.1579  +0.0403  +0.0099                  
            0.60      0.2309     +0.1677  +0.0501  +0.0197  +0.0098         
            0.80      0.2307     +0.1679  +0.0503  +0.0199  +0.0100  +0.0002

  perfect correction, averaged over the five levels with real misalignment: +0.1518

=== 3. the gradient: partial correction is nearly worthless here ===
  halving 0.20 -> 0.10: +0.0304
  halving 0.60 -> 0.30: +0.0098
  mean of the two true halvings available in the grid: +0.0201
  Halving the error from 0.60 is worth +0.0098; from 0.20 it is +0.0304.
  The payoff is concentrated at the very end of the correction, because
  the objects stop overlapping themselves long before the error is gone.

=== 4. how tight does the correction have to be? ===
  median indoor object breaks IoU@0.3 at   0.161 m
  -> required residual error               0.161 m  (sigma_pos < 0.075)
  outdoor car breaks IoU@0.3 at            1.23 m
  -> required residual error               1.23 m  (sigma_pos < 0.57)

  The indoor requirement is 7.6x tighter.
  Per class, the tightest is the fire extinguisher at 0.109 m -> 11x tighter.

  And it has to be met with fewer correspondences, not more: 53.3% of
  low-overlap frames have at most two co-visible objects, against 10.9%
  in the high-overlap split (runlogs/overlap_geometry).
